# 🛡️ TruthGuard: The Super Scout of the Internet 🕵️‍♂️

Hello there! Welcome to the **TruthGuard 2.9 Laboratory**. Imagine you heard a rumor that schools were giving out free ice cream for a whole year. Sounds awesome, right? But is it *actually* true? 🍦

TruthGuard is like a team of super-smart AI scouts who work together to find out if a story is a **fact** or just a **silly story**.

---

## 🎭 Meet the Team (The Agents)

Imagine a clubhouse where different scouts have different jobs. Here is who is inside TruthGuard:

### 1. 🧠 The Teacher (Orchestrator Agent)
The teacher is the boss. When you ask a question, the teacher tells all the other scouts what to do. At the end, the teacher listens to everyone and gives the final answer.

### 2. 📝 The Note-Taker (Claim Agent)
This scout takes a long, messy story and breaks it into small pieces. It circles the important parts like "who," "where," and "what" so the others know what to look for.

### 3. 🏃‍♂️ The Library Runner (Web Agent)
This scout is fast! They run to the "Global News Library" (the internet) and find up to 30 different newspapers and websites that are talking about your story.

### 4. 🧹 The Cleaner (Scraper Agent)
Websites can be messy with ads and pop-ups. This scout quickly reads the pages and pulls out only the clean, important words so the team doesn't get distracted by junk.

### 5. ⚖️ The Judge (Evidence Agent)
The judge compares what the Note-Taker found with what the Library Runner brought back. If 10 big newspapers say "Yes, free ice cream!" but your story says "No ice cream," the judge knows something is fishy.

### 6. ⚔️ The Debate Team (Alpha vs Omega)
This is the coolest part! Two AI agents have a friendly argument. One tries to prove the story is real, and the other tries to prove it's fake. By arguing, they find the "absolute truth" hidden in the middle.

---

## 🚀 How it Works (The Magic Steps)

1.  **You give a hint:** You type a story or paste a website link into TruthGuard.
2.  **Scouts go to work:** The team starts searching, reading, and debating all at the same time (it's very fast!).
3.  **Checking the map:** The team looks at a "Trusted Map" (a list of famous, honest news sites like BBC or Reuters) to see if they trust the people telling the story.
4.  **The Verdict:** The teacher looks at a "Truth Score."
    *   **90% - REAL:** "Yep, it's true! Here are the newspapers that said so."
    *   **10% - FAKE:** "Oops! This looks like a made-up story. Nobody honest is talking about it."
    *   **50% - SUSPICIOUS:** "Hmm, not sure yet. It might be too new or just a rumor."
